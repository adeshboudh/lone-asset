# Weapon viewmodels

Original work for this project, CC BY 4.0 (see `REUSE.toml`), except the third-party pieces named below and in `../LICENSES.md`.

## `pistol/` (slot 2)

"9mm Pistol" by TORI106 (CC BY 4.0, https://skfb.ly/owxRE) rigged together with the first-person arms ("fps arms" by DJMaesen, CC BY 4.0) in one armature, `rig/pistol_rig.blend`, built by `tools/blender/pistol_rig.py` (modes `build`, `clips`, `check`, `preview`, `export`; run `blender -b --python tools/blender/pistol_rig.py -- <mode>` with Blender 5.2). It has two hands: IK arms with control bones (`ctl_hand_L/R`, `pole_elbow_L/R`, `ctl_gun`, `ctl_mag_hold`) and the gun bones `gun_root`, `gun_slide`, `gun_mag`, `gun_mag_spare` (the fresh magazine the left hand brings in), `gun_trigger`, `gun_hammer`, `gun_muzzle`. The left hand follows the gun by the armature property `L_follows_gun` (0 to 1, keyed in the clips). The clips (Idle, Draw, Fire, Reload, Reload_Empty) live in the `.blend`; `clips --force` regenerates them from the key tables in the script and discards hand edits. `export` writes `pistol_viewmodel.glb` (deform bones only, IK baked, no textures); `pistol_viewmodel.gd` puts the textures on by material name and keeps a `Muzzle` node on the muzzle bone. `rig/` has a `.gdignore` so Godot does not import the `.blend`. The pistol does not use `FpsArms` or the M4 pack.

## `knife/` (slot 3)

"M9 Bayonet knife" by Michael Karel (CC BY 4.0, https://skfb.ly/6BY6P) is a static model without hands, so `knife_viewmodel.gd` orients and textures it and puts it in the right fist of the M4 pack's arms (`hands_rig.gd`: the rifle is hidden, the left arm shrunk away, the arm shrunk and swung down so it stays out of the sight picture). Its animations are hand-keyed clips built in code (`clip_builder.gd`, keys in the viewmodel script) that turn the arm and the knife about the grip: Draw, Idle, Fire (quick slash, fire button) and Fire_Alt (heavy thrust, aim button). The numbers are the `melee_*` fields in `data/weapons/knife.tres`.

## `m4a1_custom/` (the rifle the player uses)

"M4A1 Custom" by xlisov (fan-made art, CC-BY-NC-4.0, see `../LICENSES.md`; non-commercial, so replace it before any commercial release). `m4a1_custom_parts.glb` holds one rifle cut into separate parts, each named for the gun: `m4a1_custom_body`, `_iron_sights`, `_foregrip`, `_magazine`, `_suppressor`, `_muzzle_brake`, `_optic` and `_optic_lens`. Forward is +x and up is +y in the file. `m4a1_custom_viewmodel.gd` puts the pack's camera bone at the node origin and hangs the parts on the pack's bones: everything but the magazine on `Main_j`, the magazine on `Mag1_j` (in the gun) and a copy on `Mag2_j` (the spare in the reload). `show_suppressor(on)` swaps the suppressor and the muzzle brake for the two rifle variants. The vertical foregrip part is left off: the first-person arms' left hand holds the handguard instead, at the `LeftHandHold` node on the gun (`LEFT_HOLD_*` in `m4a1_custom_viewmodel.gd`: wrist position, hand direction, finger opening). `FpsArms` blends onto that hold while the pack's left hand is at its Idle place and lets go when an animation takes the hand away (reload). The optic is part of the model, drawn 1.25 times larger (`OPTIC_SCALE`) so the scope picture reads on screen, and its lens is scope glass: `scope_lens.gdshader` shows a picture from a second camera (`ScopeViewport`, created on the first aim and rendered only while aimed) that sits on the lens and looks where the view camera looks (the aim point under the crosshair, where the bullets go, not down the model's own barrel), zoomed by `scope_zoom` in the weapon file (1 = plain glass, no second camera). The picture is looked up from the direction the eye sees each point of the lens relative to the lens centre, so the centre always shows the aim point and moving the head shifts it a little; the reticle (a soft red dot with a faint halo, no lines), dark rim, colour fringing and glare are drawn on the glass. The HUD's plus ticks fade out as a scoped weapon is aimed (`CrosshairRules.tick_alpha`), so the dot is the only aim guide there; hit markers stay. The gun is on render layer 19 so the scope camera leaves it out (`ScopeRules`). The scope camera is moved in `frame_pre_draw`, after the animation, sway and bones have posed the lens, so the picture matches the gun on screen. Known issue under review: the picture was reported to tear in play; the lens disc is lifted 0.5 mm so it cannot fight the glass recess, and the picture has 2x MSAA. To tell whether the second camera is the cause, set `scope_zoom = 1.0` in `data/weapons/rifle_suppressed.tres` (no second camera is created) and compare. ADS puts the optic lens centre on the crosshair through `viewmodel_ads_position` in the rifle `.tres` files: x and y are the lens centre's offset from the camera axis at rest (0.0711 right, 0.0108 up, measured from the viewmodel), so change them together if the optic moves or is rescaled; a test checks the lens stays within 2 mm of the camera axis when aimed.

## `m4/` (arm rig and animations)

The arm skeleton and the animations (Idle, Draw, Fire, Reload, Reload_Empty) from "M4 - FPS Weapon Animations Pack (v.1)" by BarcodeGames (CC BY 4.0, https://skfb.ly/pNQJN). The pack's own rifle and its gloved arms are not drawn (`Hand_Mesh` is hidden); its bones still move with the animations, and the first-person arms (`content/characters/fps_arms/`, posed by `game/player/fps_arms.gd`) copy them bone by bone, fingers included. A new weapon on this rig gets arms with no extra work; a weapon on another rig needs a skeleton with the same bone names (`RightHand`, `RightHandIndex1` ...) or a new name map in `FpsArmsRules.JOINTS`. The animation names are in `data/weapons/rifle_suppressed.tres`, which also sets the hip and ADS positions. The knife uses the same rig through `hands_rig.gd`. The hand and glove textures stay only because the FBX refers to them.

## `rifle_suppressed.glb` / `.tscn`

First-person suppressed rifle, about 24,000 polygons in 5 materials (one draw call each). `source/build_rifle.py` builds it inside Blender and also exports the `.glb` and saves `source/rifle_suppressed.blend`; `source/` is ignored by Godot through `.gdignore`.

- **Body:** receiver, stock, handguard, barrel and magazine come from "M416 / HK416" by natholas, CC0 (`source/m416_blendswap_cc0.blend`, licence text beside it). The original file is an exploded layout; the script re-assembles it, rescales it to about 0.8 m and swaps the materials for ours.
- **Ours:** the suppressor and the thin open red-dot sight (two slim uprights wider apart than they are tall, red markers and a floating dot, in the style of a 1x holo sight).
- Blender +Y (forward) becomes Godot -Z. The origin sits near the trigger.
- The red dot is 0.105 m above the origin; set `viewmodel_ads_position` to (0, -0.105, -0.24) in its weapon definition to put the dot on the crosshair.
- The `.tscn` wraps the model and adds the `Muzzle` node the weapon code needs (flash light, tracer origin) at the suppressor tip.
- Materials are embedded in the `.glb`: metal, polymer, suppressor, red markers and the red dot (both emissive).
