Weapons: the player carries three weapons on keys 1, 2 and 3: the M4A1 on the developer's own arms and gun rig (`content/weapons/m4a1_ms/`, untextured, see the last section), a suppressed pistol and a combat knife. Each weapon has its own ammo and the loadout is saved. Switching is blocked while reloading or swinging (`LoadoutRules`, `Player._switch_weapon`). The knife: fire button = quick slash (45 damage), aim button = heavy thrust (80 damage, 2.3 m reach); from behind either one does 200 (silent takedown, `MeleeRules`); noise radius 3 m; numbers in `data/weapons/knife.tres`. The knife is a static model held in the M4 pack's right arm (`content/weapons/hands_rig.gd`) with hand-keyed clips built in code (`content/weapons/clip_builder.gd`; the keys are in its viewmodel script). The pistol's weapon files currently use the reference test viewmodel (see "Where we stopped"); its own rig below is kept for switching back. The pistol has its own rig: arms and pistol in one armature built in Blender (`tools/blender/pistol_rig.py`, source `content/weapons/pistol/rig/pistol_rig.blend`, export `content/weapons/pistol/pistol_viewmodel.glb`), two hands, five clips keyed in Blender; design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-17-pistol-arms-rig-design.md`, how to edit it in `content/weapons/README.md`. The crosshair stays up while aiming and its gap shows the accuracy. Sway and bob apply to every weapon. The rifle and pistol each have two versions swapped with T (0.9 s, ammo carries over, saved with the loadout; `WeaponDefinition.alt_variant`): suppressed (quiet, 14 to 18 m noise, small flash, wider spread and more recoil) and muzzle brake (`rifle_muzzle.tres`, `pistol_muzzle.tres`: tighter spread and less recoil, but an 80 m / 45 m shot noise, a loud shot sound and full flash exposure). The rifle's optic is a scope: a zoomed picture-in-picture view (`scope_zoom` 3.0 in the weapon files) with a reticle on glass, only rendered while aiming. The rifle shows the suppressor or the muzzle brake to match (`show_suppressor` in `m4a1_custom_viewmodel.gd`, also on the body copy); the pistol model is the same for both. Credits for the pistol, knife, M4 pack and M4A1 Custom (CC-BY-NC, non-commercial) are in `content/LICENSES.md`.

Player body: `PlayerBody` (`game/player/player_body.gd`, model `content/characters/agent/agent_body.glb`, credit in `content/LICENSES.md`) shows the player as a whole person for shadows and other cameras. Every mesh of the body is on a render layer the first-person camera does not draw (`PlayerBodyRules`). There are no authored animations: the pose is computed each frame. Each weapon has a body-only copy of its model (`PlayerBody.attach_guns`: shadows only, hidden from the first-person camera) that follows the real viewmodel and plays the same animation frame, so the gun in the body's hands fires, reloads and aims with the first-person one; the arms reach the wrists of that copy's own arm rig (`ArmIkRules`). `hold_right_hand` / `hold_left_hand` in `WeaponDefinition` are only the fallback when a model has no arm rig; the legs step on a foot path locked to the footstep stride and the pelvis bobs (`GaitRules`); crouching drops the pelvis, bends the knees and leans the spine (`crouch_drop_m`, `crouch_lean_deg` on the Body node). Also posed: leaning (`lean_body_deg`), sprinting (forward lean, longer lift, weapon lowered by `hold_sprint_offset`), jumping (knees tuck) with a landing dip, raising the hands when aiming (`hold_ads_offset`) and both hands to the face for binoculars (`BodyPoseState` carries the input). The body's fingers are not posed (only seen in shadows). ADS feel: `ads_time`, `ads_settle` and `ads_arc_m` in each weapon file (`AdsRules`).

First-person arms: `FpsArms` (`game/player/fps_arms.gd`, model `content/characters/fps_arms/fps_arms.glb` by DJMaesen, CC-BY; tuning in `data/player/fps_arms.tres`) is put on every weapon's arm rig by `Weapon._build_viewmodel` and copies that rig's arm, hand and finger bones every frame (maths in `FpsArmsRules`, design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-fps-arms-design.md`). The wrists sit on the rig's wrists, so every clip (fire, reload, draw) and any new weapon on the same rig drives the arms with no extra work; a rig arm scaled to nothing (the knife hides the left arm; the pistol has its own two-handed rig and gets no `FpsArms`, because `FpsArms.attach` finds no `RightHand` bone on it) folds the new arm away. The hands are scaled 1.15 (`hand_scale`) because the rig's hands are larger. The rifle's vertical foregrip is left off; its left hand holds the handguard through a hold on the gun (`LeftHandHold`, `LEFT_HOLD_*` in `m4a1_custom_viewmodel.gd`) that `FpsArms` blends in while the pack's hand is at its Idle place (`FpsArmsRules.hold_weight`) and out during the reload; the forearm takes the hand's roll (`FpsArmsRules.twist`). The third-person body's left hand still goes to the old foregrip spot (shadows only). Gun feel (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-16-procedural-viewmodel-design.md`): each shot pushes a spring that throws the gun back and up with a random roll and lets it settle with a small overshoot (`ViewmodelRecoilRules`, `recoil_kick_*` and `recoil_spring_*` in the weapon files), on top of the animation; the rifle sits further right at the hip with a slight cant (`viewmodel_hip_rotation_deg`, about `viewmodel_pivot`; its barrel points at the crosshair, a test checks it; a 13° turn-in copied from the reference looked like it shot in the wrong direction) and its left hand sits further forward. Tuned by eye against a reference recording, not yet played. The sleeves are the model's own black; retexturing them to the agent's camouflage is not done. Checked by tests and screenshots, not yet by the developer in play.

All tests pass in CI. Acceptance rows 6, 10, 11, 13 and 16 are automated. `main` is protected.

On this machine it is at `/mnt/vol1/PycharmProjects/igi3_reference/4.7_Creating_the_Reload_Animation.blend` (with the developer's reference screenshot `image.png`). Copy it to the other machine **outside the project folder**: a `.blend` inside the project makes Godot try to import it and fail with "Blender path is invalid", which also stops the import of other changed assets.
- **Regenerate the GLB:** `blender -b <path>/4.7_Creating_the_Reload_Animation.blend --python tools/blender/ref_viewmodel_export.py -- content/weapons/pistol/ref/pistol_ref_viewmodel.glb` (Blender 5.2; `--blocky-gun` keeps the reference's blocky gun). Then delete `.godot/imported/pistol_ref_viewmodel.glb-*` and run `godot --headless --path . --import`; an import cache older than the GLB makes the game show the old model. Do not run `pkill -f "godot --path"` from a shell command that contains that text: it kills its own shell (use `pkill -x godot`).
- **What the export does** (all in `tools/blender/ref_viewmodel_export.py`): replaces the blocky gun with the real pistol (scaled 1.029 to the blocky gun's length, same bottom, centre line and muzzle end, split into parts by `pistol_rig.py`, skinned to the gun rig's `gun_root`, `slide`, `mag`); bakes each game clip (Idle, Draw, Fire, Reload, Reload_Empty) with both rigs on their matching actions at once, because the hands are children of the gun and the glTF exporter bakes one rig at a time against the wrong gun clip; restores every bone to the file's pose before each bake (a bake leaves keyed values on the fingers and the next clip's constraints stack on them); scales Fire's motion to `FIRE_MOTION` = 0.3 except slide, magazine and fingers; closes the idle breath into a 60-frame loop (Idle is 59 frames so the wrap does not repeat a frame) and moves Idle onto the fire and reload rest pose so every handover lines up. Draw reuses the idle breath and Reload_Empty the reload, because the reference has neither.
- **Numbers that depend on the GLB** (re-derive after re-exporting): `MUZZLE_FROM_SLIDE` in `pistol_ref_viewmodel.gd` and in `test_pistol_ref_viewmodel.gd`; `viewmodel_ads_position` in both pistol `.tres` files = minus the idle muzzle x, and a y that puts the muzzle 2.95 cm below the screen centre (the sight tops stand 2.95 cm above the muzzle point; both sights are level). Measure the idle muzzle by instancing the viewmodel, playing Idle at 0 and reading `Muzzle.global_position`.
- **Pistol numbers set this session:** `reload_time` 2.4 s, `reload_time_empty` 2.8 s (the clip is 2.33 s; the old sounds were fitted to 1.5 and 1.9 s and now end early, not yet re-fitted); recoil roughly halved (`recoil_kick_*`) and view recoil `recoil_pitch_deg` 0.5 (0.6 suppressed), `recoil_yaw_deg` 0.2; `ads_time` 0.2.
- **ADS is the open design question.** Done and tested, but the developer says it still does not feel good and will research and come back with a design. Implemented: `Weapon.visual_aim` is a damped spring toward aiming or not (`AdsRules.spring_step`, `spring_omega`, `spring_zeta`; 95 % in `ads_time`, a small overshoot from `ads_settle` on the way up, no bounce on the way down) and drives the viewmodel position, the arc dip and the camera zoom (`Player._process`); `aim_blend` stays linear for spread, recoil and sway. `IdleSteadyModifier` (a `SkeletonModifier3D`, `game/weapons/idle_steady_modifier.gd`) pulls both skeletons toward the Idle clip's first frame by an influence the aim raises, and the idle clip slows; only Idle and Draw are steadied, never Fire or Reload. Data: `ads_idle_steady` 0.85 and `ads_idle_speed` 0.6 in the pistol files (a new `WeaponDefinition` field pair; weapons whose viewmodel has no `set_ads_steadiness` ignore them). Easy knobs: those two, `ads_time`, `ads_settle`, `ads_arc_m`, `ads_fov`.
- **Crosshair idea (suggested, not built).** Remove the crosshair and aim with ADS and optics, as in Road to Vostok. Recommended path: a Crosshair setting (Off, Dot, Full), the guard-suspicion ring kept as its own ring at the screen centre (the design draws it around the crosshair), default Off only after ADS is trusted; attachments (red dot, 2x, 4x) as a later feature (the rifle already has a 3x picture-in-picture scope, T swaps attachments; a red dot on the pistol is the cheap first one).
- **Checked: where the pistol shoots.** Bullets leave the muzzle and are aimed at the point the crosshair ray hits, so sideways they land exactly on the crosshair at any range (12.6 cm of muzzle offset is corrected). They drop below it with range: 0.8 cm at 10 m, 5 cm at 25 m, 20 cm at 50 m, 82 cm at 100 m (300 m/s, gravity scale 1.5, no drag, no compensation). Option if the crosshair goes: a per-weapon zeroing distance (about 25 m for this pistol). Hip spread is 1.6 degrees (up to about 3.4 with bloom and movement), ADS 0.12 degrees. At hip the barrel points straight ahead 12.6 cm right of the crosshair line, about 0.7 degrees at 10 m, so no inward angle is needed.
- **Next for the pistol:** the arms are the reference's blocky ones. Binding our glove skin (`content/characters/fps_arms/fps_arms.glb`) to this rig is the next step. A copy-transforms retarget of our skin onto the reference skeleton collapsed the mesh; the working approach found is the old own rig with hands sized to the gun (our hands were 0.385 m long against the reference's 0.215, scale the arms 0.56) and rolled so the finger curl axis is vertical. Either bind our skin to the reference skeleton with proper weights, or take the reference's measured wrist offsets and finger bends into `pistol_rig.py` (done once, commit `32752f5`).
- **Godot gotchas learned:** with a `SkeletonModifier3D` present, `Skeleton3D.get_bone_global_pose` read outside the `skeleton_updated` signal returns the unmodified animation pose (Godot restores it after the update); `AnimationPlayer.pause()` empties `current_animation` (use `assigned_animation`); a skeleton whose animation is paused is not re-evaluated each frame, so a modifier's influence change shows only while a clip plays.

## Branches

- `dev` is the main development track. Branch from it, open pull requests into it, merge when approved.
- `main` is the release line: it only receives merges from `dev` when a build is ready to release. Pushes to `main` refresh the `preview` release.
- CI runs on pull requests and on pushes to `main`, not on plain pushes to `dev`. Pull requests into `dev` that only touch Markdown, `docs/`, `LICENSES/` or `REUSE.toml` run only the licence check.

## How to run it

- Download the Windows or Linux zip from the [`preview` release](https://github.com/adeshboudh/lone-asset/releases/tag/preview) (refreshed on every push to `main`) or from a pull request's CI artifacts.
- Windows: unzip, run `LoneAsset.exe`; SmartScreen may warn (unsigned): More info, Run anyway.
- From source: Godot 4.7.2 (see `ENGINE_VERSION`), `godot --editor --path .`.

Controls: WASD move, Shift sprint, C crouch, Q/E lean, Space jump, mouse aim, left click fire, right click aim down sights, R reload, X fire mode, T swap suppressor / muzzle brake, 1/2/3 rifle/pistol/knife, F interact (hold at terminal), B binoculars (wheel zooms), Tab inventory, F3 debug views, Esc pause.

Weapon switching and muzzle effects (2026-11-22): there are no draw or holster clips in the supplied rigs, so both are procedural in `Weapon._process`: a switch first lowers and tilts the weapon in hand for `HOLSTER_TIME_S` (0.2 s, no firing meanwhile; `Weapon.begin_holster`, `Player._switch_weapon`), then raises the new one (`EQUIP_TIME_S`).
